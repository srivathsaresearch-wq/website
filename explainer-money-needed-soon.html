Money you need soonneeds a different home.

Markets can fall at any time and take years to recover. Money for next year's fees or a wedding shouldn't have to wait for that.

4 min read · Updated 17 October 2026

In short

  • The sooner you need the money, the less time it has to recover from a fall.
  • Short-term money usually belongs somewhere steady and easy to reach, even if it earns less.
  • Build an emergency fund first. Then invest money you won't need for years.

The problem with short timelines

Equity can grow over long periods, but it can also fall sharply in a single year and stay down for a while. If you invest money you need in eighteen months and markets fall just before then, you face a hard choice: sell at a loss, or give up what you were saving for.

Time is what lets you wait out a fall. Short-term money doesn't have it.

Sort your money by when you'll need it

  • Soon: your emergency fund, upcoming fees, a planned purchase.
  • In a few years: a down payment, a vehicle, a wedding.
  • Many years away: retirement, or a child's education far ahead.

The longer the timeline, the more ups and downs you can usually live with. The shorter it is, the more steadiness matters.

Where short-term money often goes

These aren't recommendations, just the kinds of places people commonly use:

  • Savings accounts and fixed deposits with a bank.
  • Recurring deposits, for saving a set amount each month.
  • Debt funds designed for short periods, such as liquid funds. These can still lose value, though usually far less than equity.

Each has trade-offs in return, access and tax. None will grow much, and that is fine. Their job is to be there when you need them (see inflation, in plain words for the cost of holding cash for long).

The emergency fund comes first

Life brings surprises: a job loss, a medical bill, a family need. Without a cushion, people end up selling investments at a bad time, or borrowing at high rates.

A common rule of thumb is to keep a few months of essential expenses somewhere safe and easy to reach before investing for the long term. The right amount depends on how steady your income is and who depends on you.

Questions to ask before investing

  • When will I need this money?
  • If it fell a lot just before then, what would I do?
  • Do I have an emergency fund?
  • Is any of this money borrowed?
