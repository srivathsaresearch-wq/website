Stories

Three short stories, three lessons

These are made-up stories to explain common situations. They are not about real people.

Illustrative story

The tip that cost a month's salary

A colleague at work gets a message in a WhatsApp group: a small company's share is “about to double”. Everyone in the group seems sure. The colleague puts in money meant for the month's bills, without reading anything about the company. For a few days the price rises and the group celebrates. Then it falls hard, well below what they paid. The people who sent the tip go quiet. Selling now locks in the loss. Holding means hoping. Either way, a month's salary is gone, and nobody in the group asked the basic questions.

Lesson: tips skip the questions that matter. Ask first: who is telling me this, and what do they gain? What does the business actually do? How much could I lose? How would I get out?

Illustrative story

The fee you never see

Two investors each put away ₹1,00,000 [hypothetical] in investments that behave exactly the same way. The only difference is cost. One pays 0.5% a year [hypothetical]; the other pays 2% a year [hypothetical]. The costs come out of the fund quietly, so neither ever sees a bill. To show the effect of costs alone, imagine the investment itself doesn't grow or fall. After 20 years [hypothetical], the first would have about ₹90,500 and the second about ₹66,800 [hypothetical]. That's a gap of about ₹23,700, just from fees.

Lesson: small costs compound too. Check the yearly cost (for a mutual fund, the expense ratio) before you invest, and compare like with like.

Illustrative story

The day everyone panicked

Picture a crisis. The news is everywhere and markets fall sharply, day after day. A first-time investor opens their app and sees their savings lower than they ever imagined. Friends are selling. Headlines say it could get worse. Selling feels like the safe choice, so they sell. Later, things calm down and prices pick up again, but they are still sitting in cash, waiting for it to “feel safe”. Markets don't always recover, and never on a timetable. But a fall on paper becomes a real loss the moment you sell in fear.

Lesson: decide your plan before the panic. Know when you'll need the money, how big a fall you could live with, and what you'll do if it happens. Write it down.

Equity, debt and gold: what each one is for

A portfolio is a mix. Each part has a different job.

02 · Equity

Owning a small piece of businesses.

Can grow over long periods. Can also fall a lot in the short term.

03 · Debt

Lending money and being paid interest.

Usually steadier than equity. Still carries risks, like the borrower not paying.

04 · Gold

A metal people trust when other things wobble.

Pays no interest. Often behaves differently from shares.

05 · Cash

Money you can reach today.

Safe from market swings. Slowly loses buying power to inflation.

The right mix depends on you: your goals, timeline and comfort with ups and downs.
