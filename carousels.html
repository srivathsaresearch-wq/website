What a falling rupee means for your money

1 / 8
01

The rupee “falls” when it buys fewer dollars than before.

Swipe to see where you might feel it.

02 · What it means

The same dollar now costs more rupees.

An exchange rate is a price, like any other. It moves a little every day.

03 · Imports

Things India buys from abroad get dearer.

Fuel, electronics and some medicines are bought in dollars. Part of that can show up in everyday prices.

04 · Travel and study

Foreign trips, fees and dollar subscriptions cost more.

The bill in dollars is the same. It takes more rupees to pay it.

05 · Exporters

Some businesses earn more rupees.

Companies that sell abroad are paid in dollars, which now turn into more rupees.

06 · Money invested abroad

Foreign investments can be worth more in rupees.

A falling rupee can lift their rupee value. A rising rupee can shrink it. Currency is a risk both ways.

07 · The RBI

The RBI watches the rupee, but doesn't fix it at one level.

It can step in to calm sharp swings. Day to day, the market sets the rate.

08

A falling rupee isn't simply good or bad. What matters is what you spend, earn and save in.

This is education, not advice.

Equity, debt and gold: what each one is for

1 / 6
01

A portfolio is a mix. Each part has a different job.

Swipe to see what each one does.

02 · Equity

Owning a small piece of businesses.

Can grow over long periods. Can also fall a lot in the short term.

03 · Debt

Lending money and being paid interest.

Usually steadier than equity. Still carries risks, like the borrower not paying.

04 · Gold

A metal people trust when other things wobble.

Pays no interest. Often behaves differently from shares.

05 · Cash

Money you can reach today.

Safe from market swings. Slowly loses buying power to inflation.

06

The right mix depends on you: your goals, timeline and comfort with ups and downs.

This is education, not advice.

SIP or lump sum: what's the difference?

1 / 7
01

Two ways to put money in: a little every month, or all at once.

Swipe to see how each one works.

02 · SIP

A SIP puts in a fixed amount on a fixed date, every month.

SIP stands for systematic investment plan. It's a way of paying in, not a product of its own.

03 · Lump sum

A lump sum puts the whole amount in on one day.

It often comes up when a large amount arrives at once, like a bonus.

04 · Buying at many prices

With a SIP, you buy at lots of different prices.

When prices are lower, the same amount buys more units; when higher, fewer. This evens out your cost. It doesn't guarantee a gain.

05 · Timing

With a lump sum, the day you invest matters more.

If prices fall soon after, the whole amount feels it. If they rise, the whole amount gains.

06 · Habit

Many people choose a SIP because it matches a monthly salary.

It builds a habit and takes the guesswork out of “when”.

07

Neither is always better. It depends on how your money arrives, and how you'd feel if prices fell next week.

This is education, not advice.

Seven red flags of a “tips” group

1 / 9
01

Tips groups on WhatsApp and Telegram can look convincing.

Here are seven warning signs. Swipe through.

02 · Red flag 1

“Guaranteed” or “sure-shot” returns.

Nobody can honestly promise returns. Investments can fall.

03 · Red flag 2

Pressure to act right now.

“Buy before 3 pm.” “Last chance.” Urgency stops you asking questions.

04 · Red flag 3

Screenshots of profits as proof.

Screenshots are easy to fake, and they only ever show the wins.

05 · Red flag 4

A fee to join the “premium” or “VIP” group.

The free group is often there to sell you the paid one.

06 · Red flag 5

Asking for your login or OTP, or to trade for you.

Never share your trading login, OTP or UPI PIN. Not with anyone.

07 · Red flag 6

Small, little-known shares “about to double”.

Shares that hardly trade are easy to push up, and can fall just as fast when the senders sell.

08 · Red flag 7

Questions get you told off, or removed.

Honest teaching welcomes questions, especially awkward ones.

09

If it sounds too good to be true, step back and ask: who gains if I act on this?
