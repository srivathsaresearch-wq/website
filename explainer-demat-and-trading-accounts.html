Demat and trading:two accounts, two jobs.

To buy shares or ETFs in India you need both. One holds what you own; the other places your orders.

3 min read · Updated 17 October 2026

The demat account: where things are held The trading account: where orders are placed Do you need them for mutual funds? What opening one involves Keeping it safe

In short

  • A demat account holds shares and other securities electronically, in your name.
  • A trading account is what you use to place buy and sell orders on an exchange.
  • They are usually opened together through a broker. Check the charges, and never share your login details.

The demat account: where things are held

Shares used to be paper certificates. Now they are held electronically, or “dematerialised”, in a demat account. Think of it like a bank account that holds shares, bonds, ETFs and sometimes mutual fund units instead of money.

The account sits with one of India's two depositories, through an intermediary called a depository participant. Very often that is your broker.

The trading account: where orders are placed

To buy or sell on a stock exchange, you place an order through a broker using a trading account. When you buy, money leaves your linked bank account and the shares arrive in your demat account once the trade settles. When you sell, the reverse happens.

Do you need them for mutual funds?

Not always. You can usually invest in mutual funds directly with the fund house, or through a platform, without a demat account. You do need demat and trading accounts for shares and ETFs (see index funds and active funds).

What opening one involves

  • KYC: identity and address proof, your PAN, and a bank account in your name. Often done online, with a short video or in-person check.
  • Charges: look at the opening fee, the yearly maintenance charge, the brokerage on each trade, and the other small charges added to every trade. Each is small; with frequent trading they add up.

Keeping it safe

  • Never share your login, PIN or one-time passwords, even with someone offering to “manage” your account for you (see red flags of a tips group).
  • Turn on alerts so you get a message for every trade and every change to your details.
  • Read the consolidated statement the depository sends you. It lists what you hold, independently of your broker.
